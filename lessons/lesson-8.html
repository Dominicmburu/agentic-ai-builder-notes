Week 2 · Lesson 8 · Integration

Connecting to Google Docs and Gmail

Choose the right way to log in, then build an agent that reads a document and drafts an email.

About 45 minutes to read Integration Download the slides

The big idea

To use your own documents and email, n8n needs permission to get in. There are three ways to give that permission, from easy to hard. Pick the easiest one that works, and keep a human in the loop.

By the end you can:

  • Choose the right authentication pattern for an integration.
  • Connect Google Drive or Docs and Gmail to an agent.
  • Build an agent that reads a document and drafts an email reply.

Three ways to log in

Authentication just means proving who you are and what you are allowed to do. Think of getting into a building.

LevelPatternWhat it isEveryday comparison
EasyAPI keyA simple password-style key you copy from the service and paste into n8nA house key. Whoever holds it can enter
MediumPre-configured OAuth2 loginYou click “Sign in with Google”. n8n has already done the hard setup for you. Google Sheets and Gmail work this wayA hotel keycard issued at reception. You approve it, and it can be cancelled
HardFull OAuth2 for a custom integrationYou register your own app with the service and set up the login yourself. It takes patienceApplying to the council for your own keycard system

OAuth2 is the “Sign in with Google” style of login. You never hand n8n your password. You approve a limited permission instead, and you can take it back at any time.

Good to know Integrations are optional. Most services have a free tier. If you prefer a different service for storing documents or sending email, you can choose another. The ideas are the same.

Stay safe while you experiment

  • Use a test document and, if you can, a spare or test email account.
  • Make the workflow create a draft. Do not send anything until you have read it.
  • Give the smallest permissions that do the job.
  • When you finish the course, remove the n8n access in your Google account settings.
Why drafts matter An AI can make a mistake and write something wrong, rude or private. A draft sits in your folder waiting for you. A sent email cannot be unsent.

Build it: read a document, draft an email

  1. Prepare a test document in Google Docs: a short note, a meeting summary or a customer message. A page or less is perfect.
  2. Add a start node. A manual trigger is easiest while testing.
  3. Add the Google Docs node with the operation to get a document. Click Sign in with Google to connect, then give the document's link or ID. (You can use the Google Drive node to download a file instead.)
  4. Add an AI Agent with a chat model. In its prompt, pass in the document text. In n8n you do this with an expression, which is a small piece of code in double curly braces that pulls in data from an earlier node. You learn expressions properly in Lesson 9.
  5. Add the Gmail node, choose the draft resource and the create operation. Set a subject, and set the message to the agent's output.
  6. Run it by hand. Open Gmail and read the draft before doing anything else.
Prompt for the agentYou write short, polite email replies. Read the document below. Then write: 1. A summary in three sentences. 2. A draft email to the sender, in plain friendly language. Use only facts from the document. If something is unclear, say so in the email instead of guessing. DOCUMENT: (the document text goes here, inserted by n8n)
Where to find the agent's answer The AI Agent node puts its answer in a field called output. To use it in the Gmail message, switch the field to expression mode and enter {{ $json.output }}.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it usually means
Google shows a warning screen while connectingRead it carefully. Use your own account, approve only the permissions needed, and continue only if you started the connection yourself.
“Not found” for the documentWrong link or ID, or your Google account has no access to it.
The draft says undefined or is emptyThe expression points to the wrong field. Click the previous node and look at its output.
The summary invents thingsAdd “use only facts from the document” and a rule to say when something is unclear.

Quick quiz

Click an answer. You will see right away if it was correct, and why.

1. Which authentication pattern is a simple password-style key?

An API key is the easy pattern: copy it from the service and keep it secret.

2. In n8n cloud, how do Google Sheets and Gmail usually connect?

That is the medium pattern. You click sign in and approve the permission.

3. Why should the workflow create a draft instead of sending the email?

AI can make mistakes. A draft keeps a human in control.

4. Which pattern needs the most patience?

You set up the registration and login yourself.

5. Must you use Google for these integrations?

The patterns apply to any service. Most have free tiers.

Glossary

Authentication
Proving who you are and what you are allowed to do.
API key
A simple password-style credential copied from a service. Keep it secret.
OAuth2
The 'Sign in with Google' style of login. You approve limited permissions without sharing your password.
Pre-configured OAuth2
OAuth2 where n8n has already done the hard setup. You just click sign in.
Integration
A connection between n8n and another service, such as Gmail or Google Drive.
Draft
An email saved but not sent.
Expression
A small piece of code in double curly braces that pulls data into a field. Explained in Lesson 9.

Recap and what comes next

  1. Three ways to log in: API key (easy), pre-configured OAuth2 (medium), full OAuth2 (hard). Pick the easiest that works.
  2. Use test documents and create drafts. Read everything before anything is sent.
  3. A document-to-draft workflow is four nodes: start, read, AI agent, Gmail draft.
