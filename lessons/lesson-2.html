Tokens: how an AI reads, and why it matters

Tokenization and how generation works, in plain language.

About 40 minutes to read Core Download the slides

The big idea

An AI does not read words. It reads small chunks called tokens, and it writes one token at a time. Tokens decide what you pay, how fast you get an answer, and how much the AI can handle at once.

By the end you can:

  • Explain what a token is and estimate how many are in a piece of text.
  • Explain why the number of tokens drives cost, speed and limits.
  • Explain temperature, the dial that makes an AI predictable or adventurous.

What is a token?

Everyday comparison: Lego bricks You can build a sentence out of whole words, like big Lego blocks. An AI builds it from small bricks instead. Common words are usually one brick. Rare or long words are snapped from several bricks. Spaces, punctuation and numbers are bricks too.

A word like tea is probably one token. A long or unusual word such as unbelievable might be split into a few pieces. The exact splits are different for each AI family, so do not memorise them. Just remember: tokens are the units the AI counts in.

Why does this matter to you? Everything is priced and limited in tokens, not words. Even the thing you type and the answer you get back are measured this way.

The four-characters rule

For ordinary English, one token is about four characters, or roughly three-quarters of a word. So 100 tokens is around 75 words.

It is only an estimate The rule changes with the language, with code, and with the model. Swahili, Arabic, Chinese and computer code usually need more tokens for the same meaning. That means the same message can cost more in some languages.

Why tokens matter: cost, speed and limits

Cost

Like phone airtime. You pay for tokens you send (input) and tokens the AI writes (output). The two often have different prices.

Speed

The AI writes one token at a time, like someone typing. A long answer takes longer than a short one.

Limits

The context window is the AI's suitcase. Everything you send plus the answer must fit inside it.

A worked cost example

Say a model charges $1 per million input tokens and $4 per million output tokens. You send 2,000 tokens and get 500 back.

PartSumCost
Input2,000 / 1,000,000 × $1$0.002
Output500 / 1,000,000 × $4$0.002
One call$0.004
10,000 such calls$0.004 × 10,000$40

Tiny for one call, real money at scale. This is why builders care about keeping prompts short and choosing the right model.

Everyday comparison: the suitcase The context window is like an airline luggage limit. Your question, the earlier conversation and the answer all share one suitcase. If it is full, something has to be left behind.

How the AI picks each token

Last lesson you saw that an AI guesses what comes next. Here is the detail. At every step, the AI works out a probability for every possible next token, like a scoreboard. Then it picks one, adds it, and repeats.

For I would like a cup of ... the scoreboard might look like: tea 46%, coffee 41%, water 7%, and a long tail of other words with tiny chances. There is no plan and no fact lookup. Each pick comes from the scoreboard.

Temperature: the creativity dial

Temperature is a setting that reshapes the scoreboard before the pick.

SettingWhat happensGood for
Low (0 to 0.3)Nearly always picks the favourite. Predictable, repeatable.Sorting emails, pulling out facts, anything a program must read
High (0.8 and above)Gives long shots a real chance. Varied and surprising.Slogans, brainstorming, stories
Everyday comparison: ordering lunch Low temperature is the person who orders the same meal every time. High temperature is the person who tries something new each visit.
Two common mistakes Low temperature does not mean correct. An AI can be consistently wrong. And even at 0, two runs are not guaranteed to be identical. Also, some reasoning models ignore this setting.

Fluent is not the same as true

Remember: wrong answers are produced in exactly the same way as right ones. The AI picks likely tokens, and nothing in that process checks the facts. That is why it can sound confident and still be wrong. You will learn ways to reduce this later in the course: giving the AI your own documents (Week 3) and testing its output (every project).

Part 2: temperature 0 and temperature 1

Pick a chat tool that lets you set the temperature (the OpenRouter chat page has a settings panel for this, and many other tools do too). Send this prompt three times at temperature 0, then three times at temperature 1, and compare:

“Write a one-sentence slogan for a coffee shop in Nairobi.”

Write two sentences: what changed between the two settings, and which setting you would use to sort customer emails into categories.

For the curious: do part 2 with a short Python script

This sends the same prompt three times at each temperature. Use a chat model, not a reasoning model.

import os
import requests

API_KEY = os.environ["OPENROUTER_API_KEY"]
URL = "https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions"
MODEL = "MODEL_ID"  # copy a chat model name from openrouter.ai/models
PROMPT = "Write a one-sentence slogan for a coffee shop in Nairobi."

def ask(temperature):
    resp = requests.post(
        URL,
        headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {API_KEY}", "Content-Type": "application/json"},
        json={"model": MODEL, "temperature": temperature,
              "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": PROMPT}]},
        timeout=60,
    )
    return resp.json()["choices"][0]["message"]["content"].strip()

for t in (0, 1):
    print(f"--- temperature {t} ---")
    for i in range(3):
        print(f"{i + 1}. {ask(t)}")

To count tokens in code instead of a website, run pip install tiktoken and use tiktoken.get_encoding("cl100k_base").encode(text). The length of the result is the token count. Other model families count slightly differently.

Quick quiz

Click an answer. You will see right away if it was correct, and why.

1. What is a token?

Tokens are the units an AI counts in. Cost, speed and limits are all measured with them.

2. In ordinary English, roughly how many characters make one token?

It is a rough rule of thumb: about four characters, or three-quarters of a word.

3. Which tokens do you usually pay for?

Input and output are both billed, often at different prices. Both also count toward the context window.

4. You want an AI to sort emails into categories the same way every time. Which temperature suits best?

Low temperature gives predictable, repeatable output, which is what sorting needs.

5. An AI is set to temperature 0. What does that guarantee?

Temperature changes how adventurous the pick is. It cannot check facts, so a wrong answer can be repeated consistently.

Glossary

Token
A small chunk of text, often a word or part of a word, that an AI reads and writes. Everything is counted in tokens.
Tokenizer
The tool that splits text into tokens. Each model family has its own.
Input tokens
The tokens you send to the AI, including your instructions and earlier conversation.
Output tokens
The tokens the AI writes back.
Context window
The maximum number of tokens an AI can handle in one call, input and output together.
Temperature
A setting that controls how predictable or varied the AI's picks are. Low is predictable, high is varied.

Recap and what comes next

  1. A token is a small chunk of text. About four English characters make one token.
  2. Tokens decide cost (you pay per token), speed (one token at a time) and limits (the context window).
  3. Temperature is the dial between predictable and adventurous. Low for tasks, high for creativity. Neither guarantees truth.
