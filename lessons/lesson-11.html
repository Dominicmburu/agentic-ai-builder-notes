Voice agents with ElevenLabs

Build an AI that you can talk to, and find where it gets slow or goes off topic.

About 40 minutes to read Core Download the slides

The big idea

A voice agent is the same AI you already know, with ears and a mouth added. It listens, thinks, then speaks. What makes it feel good or bad is speed and turn-taking.

By the end you can:

  • Explain the difference between ElevenLabs' creative platform and its agents platform.
  • Create a conversational voice agent with a clear prompt and a chosen voice.
  • Test the agent live and identify where it is slow or goes off topic.

What is ElevenLabs?

ElevenLabs is a company, founded in 2022, that is a leader in generating realistic audio. It has two parts that are easy to mix up:

Creative platform

Makes audio. You give it text and it reads it aloud in a chosen voice, like a voice-over artist you can hire by the second.

Agents platform

Runs a live conversation. It listens, thinks with a language model, and talks back, like a receptionist.

Pricing: there is a generous free tier, and paid plans are monthly. Check the pricing page for current limits before you plan a big test.

How a voice agent works: ears, brain, mouth

Two things decide whether it feels natural:

  • Latency. The pause between you finishing and the agent starting to answer. Long pauses feel awkward, like a bad phone line.
  • Turn-taking. Knowing when you are done speaking and when you want to interrupt.
Everyday comparison: a receptionist A good receptionist answers quickly, speaks in short sentences, does not read out a whole catalogue, and stays on the subject. That is exactly what we want from a voice agent.

Writing a prompt for voice

A prompt for voice differs from a prompt for chat. Spoken answers must be short and direct, because long ones sound unnatural and take longer to say.

  • Ask for short sentences and a maximum length.
  • Ask for one question at a time.
  • No bullet points, emoji, or formatting symbols. They sound strange when read aloud.
  • Tell it what to do when it does not know.
  • Give it a first message, the greeting it says when a call begins.
Example system promptYou are the phone assistant for Hillside Bakery in Nyeri. Your job is to answer questions about opening hours, the menu and how to order. Speak in short, friendly sentences. Keep every answer under three sentences. Ask only one question at a time. Never read out long lists. Give the two most useful items and offer to say more. If you do not know something, say "I'm not sure, but I can take a message." Only talk about the bakery. If asked about anything else, politely say you can only help with the bakery.
Example first messageHello, thanks for calling Hillside Bakery. How can I help you today?

Build your first voice agent

  1. Create an account on the ElevenLabs website and open the Agents area.
  2. Choose Create an agent and give it a name.
  3. Paste your first message and your system prompt.
  4. Pick a voice that suits the job. Listen to a few. Warm and clear usually beats dramatic.
  5. Choose the language, and the language model if the settings offer a choice. Faster models usually mean lower latency.
  6. Use the test button to talk to it in your browser. Allow the microphone when your browser asks.
Menus change ElevenLabs updates its interface often. If a button has moved or has a new name, look for the same idea in the agent settings.

Your exercise

Build a voice agent that answers three scripted questions about a topic you choose (a bakery, a bicycle shop, a school, a salon). Time each response and note any answer that drifts off topic.

  1. Write three questions in advance, for example: “What time do you open on Saturday?” “Do you make birthday cakes?” “What is the cheapest thing on your menu?”
  2. Ask each one aloud. Use a phone stopwatch to time from when you stop talking until the agent starts answering.
  3. Note whether the answer was on topic, correct for the information you gave it, and short enough.
  4. Fix one problem in the prompt and ask the same questions again.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it usually means
It cannot hear youThe browser blocked the microphone. Allow it in the address bar settings, then reload.
Long pausesA slow language model, a long prompt or a poor connection. Shorten the prompt and try a faster model.
It ramblesAdd a clear length limit and a rule to give the top two items only.
It talks over you, or waits too longTurn-taking settings need adjusting. Try speaking more clearly and check the agent's interruption settings.
It makes up an answerAdd a rule for what to say when it does not know. Week 3 shows how to give it real data.

Glossary

ElevenLabs
A company that makes realistic AI audio. It offers a creative platform and an agents platform.
Creative platform
ElevenLabs' tools for generating audio, such as reading text aloud in a chosen voice.
Agents platform
ElevenLabs' tools for running a live voice conversation with an AI.
Speech to text
Turning spoken audio into written words. The agent's ears.
Text to speech
Turning written words into spoken audio. The agent's mouth.
Latency
The delay between you finishing speaking and the agent starting to answer.
Turn-taking
Knowing when one speaker has finished so the other can start, including interruptions.
First message
The greeting a voice agent says when a conversation begins.

Recap and what comes next

  1. A voice agent adds ears (speech to text) and a mouth (text to speech) to the language model brain.
  2. Latency and turn-taking decide whether it feels natural. Short, direct prompts usually sound better.
  3. Test with fixed questions, time each answer, and fix one problem at a time.
