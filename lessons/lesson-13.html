RAG and agentic RAG

How an AI answers from your own documents instead of guessing.

About 45 minutes to read Core Download the slides

The big idea

RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. In plain words: before the AI answers, it looks up the right pages in your documents, then answers using only those pages.

By the end you can:

  • Explain embeddings, chunking and similarity search in simple terms.
  • Describe the two phases of RAG: ingestion and answering.
  • Explain how agentic RAG differs from standard RAG.

Why do we need it?

A language model only knows what it learned in training. It has never seen your price list, your policies or your product sheets. If you ask, it may guess, and a confident guess is the worst kind of wrong answer.

Everyday comparison: an open-book exam A closed-book exam tests memory. An open-book exam lets the student find the right page, then answer from it. RAG turns your AI into an open-book student, and the book is your documents.

Embeddings: turning meaning into numbers

A computer cannot compare meaning directly. So we turn each piece of text into a long list of numbers called an embedding. Texts with similar meaning get similar numbers.

Everyday comparison: a map Imagine a map where every sentence is a pin. “How do I return shoes?” and “What is your refund policy?” land close together. “Best pizza in town” lands far away. The numbers are the pin's coordinates.

To find relevant text, we turn the question into a pin too, and fetch the nearest pins. This is called similarity search. Try it:

Real embeddings have hundreds or thousands of numbers, not two, but the idea is the same: close together means similar meaning.

Chunking: cutting documents into pieces

You do not search whole documents. You cut them into chunks, short passages, and give each one an embedding.

  • Too big: a chunk covers many topics, so the match is vague.
  • Too small: a chunk loses its context, such as a price with no product name.
  • Good starting point: a few sentences or one short section, often with a small overlap between neighbours.
Everyday comparison: index cards Instead of handing someone a whole textbook, you write each idea on its own index card. Finding the right card is quick. But a card that just says “KES 450” is useless.

The two phases

One rule that catches everyone The question and the documents must be embedded with the same model. Mix them and the map no longer lines up, so the search returns nonsense.

Standard RAG and agentic RAG

Standard RAGAgentic RAG
Who decides to search?The system. It always searches once, then answers.The agent. It chooses whether to search, what to search for, and whether to search again.
Number of searchesExactly oneZero, one or several
Good forSimple, predictable questionsVague or multi-part questions, small talk, comparing two items
Cost and speedFaster and cheaperSlower and costlier, more flexible
Everyday comparison: two librarians The first librarian always walks to one shelf and brings back whatever is there. The second thinks first: “Do I need a book? Which shelf? Is this enough, or should I check another?” That second librarian is agentic RAG. In n8n, the search is simply a tool you give the agent, as in Lesson 4.

Example: a customer asks, “Compare the Basic and Pro blender, and tell me which is quieter.” An agentic system searches for Basic, then searches for Pro, then compares. A one-shot system may only retrieve one of them.

Making the AI stay honest

Grounding rules for the promptAnswer only using the information returned by the search tool. If the search results do not contain the answer, say "I don't have that information" and offer to take a message. Never use outside knowledge for prices, specifications or policies. When you give a price or a specification, say which product it belongs to.

Without such rules the model happily fills gaps from its own memory, which is exactly what you were trying to stop.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it usually means
The answer is wrong but sounds confidentRetrieval returned the wrong chunks, or the model ignored them. Check what was retrieved first.
It answers things not in your documentsThe grounding rules are missing or too weak.
Prices are attached to the wrong productChunks lost context. Put the product name inside every chunk.
The search returns irrelevant textDifferent embedding models were used, or the chunks are too big or too small.

Quick quiz

Click an answer. You will see right away if it was correct, and why.

1. What does RAG add to a language model?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation means retrieve first, then generate the answer.

2. What is an embedding?

Similar meanings give similar numbers.

3. Why do we cut documents into chunks?

Chunks are like index cards: quick to find and focused on one idea.

4. What must stay the same between ingestion and answering?

A different embedding model means a different map, so the search no longer works.

5. What makes RAG agentic?

In agentic RAG the search is a tool the agent chooses to use.

Glossary

RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Look up relevant text first, then answer from it.
Embedding
A list of numbers that stands for the meaning of a piece of text.
Chunk
A short passage cut from a larger document, searched and retrieved on its own.
Similarity search
Finding the stored chunks whose embeddings are closest to the question's.
Vector store
A database that holds embeddings and can search them by closeness.
Ingestion
The one-off preparation phase: cut documents into chunks, embed them, store them.
Agentic RAG
RAG where the agent chooses when and how often to search.
Grounding
Making the AI answer only from the retrieved information.
Hallucination
A confident answer that is made up.

Recap and what comes next

  1. RAG lets the AI answer from your documents: look up the right chunks first, then answer using only them.
  2. Embeddings turn meaning into numbers. Similarity search finds the nearest chunks. Use the same embedding model in both phases.
  3. Ingestion happens ahead of time. Answering happens on every question. Agentic RAG lets the agent decide when to search.
